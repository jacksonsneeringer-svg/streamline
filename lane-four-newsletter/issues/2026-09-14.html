In the Water This Week
McKeown lost her 50 back WR. She's not taking it lying down.
Kaylee McKeown had her 50-meter backstroke world record broken by Sara Curtis this summer, and her reaction was about as fierce as you'd expect: she says it fuels the fire and she's all for taking people down. (via SwimSwam)
Brendan Hansen tore both patellar tendons in a cannonball contest
Six-time Olympic medalist and USA Swimming National Junior Team Director Brendan Hansen tore both patellar tendons during a July 4th cannonball contest and is now five weeks into rehab. A reminder that the pool is sometimes the safest place to be. (via SwimSwam)
Kate Douglass has a number in mind: 22-point-something
Kate Douglass confirmed in a SwimSwam podcast with Mel Stewart that breaking the 23-second barrier in the 50-meter freestyle is on the table, and she's already mapping the road to LA 2028. (via SwimSwam)

The University of Texas will get a December head start with top-ranked 2027 recruit Luka Mijatovic and highly rated Ian Call both arriving on campus before the new year. (via SwimSwam)
Athlete of the Week

Sara Curtis
50m Back World Record NCAA Champion
Virginia Cavalier 2026 Breakout Star

Sara Curtis, born on 19 August 2006 in Savigliano, Italy, is an Italian competitive swimmer specializing in sprint freestyle and backstroke events. She was born to an Italian father and a Nigerian mother and trains under coach Thomas Maggiora with Team Dimensione Nuoto.

Curtis holds the long course world record in the 50 metre backstroke. At the 2022 World Junior Championships she won silver medals in the 50 metre freestyle, 4x100 metre medley relay, and 4x100 metre mixed medley relay, along with a bronze in the 50 metre backstroke. At the 2022 European Junior Championships she won gold in the 4x100 metre freestyle relay and bronze medals in the 50 metre freestyle and 4x100 metre medley relay.
