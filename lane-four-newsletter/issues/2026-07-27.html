In the Water This Week
Australia breaks 4x200 free Commonwealth record
Australia won gold in the men's 4x200 free relay with a new Commonwealth Games record of 7:01.67. Kai Taylor anchored with a 1:44.10 to overtake England's Max Litchfield at the wall, while James Guy threw down the fastest split in the field at 1:44.03 on the third leg for England's silver. (via SwimSwam)
15-year-old Kennedy Brooks wins twice at NCSAs
Kennedy Brooks, just 15 years old, closed out the NCSAs with a double on the final night, winning the 50 back in 28.82 and the 50 free in 26.05. That 50 free was a 0.76-second personal drop, which at any age is a big deal. (via SwimSwam)
Day 4 prelims: O'Callaghan, Scott, Southam roll on
Mollie O'Callaghan, Duncan Scott, and Flynn Southam all carried momentum into the latter half of the 2026 Commonwealth Games through Day 4 prelims. The Games are hitting their stride with some of the top names in the sport still with finals to go. (via SwimSwam)
CAS upholds two-year ban on Mexican federation ex-president
The Court of Arbitration for Sport confirmed a two-year ban on Kiril Todorov, former president of the Mexican Swimming Federation. Todorov will remain ineligible until October 28, 2026. (via SwimSwam)
Athlete of the Week

Cameron McEvoy
World Record 50m free Olympic Gold 4x100 free relay
Commonwealth Games medalist Australian national champion

Cameron McEvoy is an Australian competitive swimmer, born on 13 May 1994 on the Gold Coast. He learned to swim at age five and began training at age seven with the Miami Swimming Club. He has represented Australia at four Olympic Games, spanning from 2012 through 2024, with his main event shifting over time from the 100 m freestyle to the 50 m freestyle.

McEvoy is the reigning Olympic champion in the men's 50 m freestyle and the first Australian man to win an Olympic medal in that event. He holds the world record in the long course 50 m freestyle. His other achievements include a silver medal at the 2015 World Championships in the 100 m freestyle, world titles in the 50 m freestyle in 2023 and 2025, and multiple Commonwealth Games medals.

Gear Drops & Swim Tech
Gear of the Week
FINIS Freestyler paddle with skeg
The specialist. Its elongated shape and bottom skeg act like a keel — cross over your centerline on entry and the paddle tracks crooked so you feel the error instantly.
FINIS Long Floating Fins for beginners: The exception to the short-blade rule, and the fin nearly every swimmer starts on. The extra length and float give true beginners the propulsion and flotation to learn the kicking motion.
FINIS Agility strapless swim paddles: The smartest paddle on the market — no strap, just a thumb hole, so it only stays on if you keep constant pressure on the water with a proper catch. Go lazy and it falls off.
Speedo Team Pull Buoy: The bulk-bin workhorse — cheap, durable, and perfectly functional. It survives being thrown in a bag, stepped on, and left in a hot car all summer, which is why clubs buy it by the dozen.
